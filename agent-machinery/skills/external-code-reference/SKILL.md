# External material: full notes

## Permission precedes derivation

Public visibility is not permission to copy or adapt. Inspect the license at
the exact revision and determine whether it covers the material in question,
including bundled assets and examples. A repository license may exclude
third-party payloads. Attribution does not cure missing permission.

Retain required copyright, license, and NOTICE material for permissive sources.
Copyleft, source-available, or otherwise restrictive terms require an explicit
compatibility decision for the intended use. An unidentified or absent license
does not become MIT because a local importer supplies that default.

## What kind of use is intended?

- Copying, translation, light editing, and structural adaptation may retain
  protected expression and need the relevant permission.
- Facts, ideas, and observed behavior can inform independent work, but calling
  an adaptation “inspiration” does not make it independent.
- Linking, distribution, hosted use, and modifications can have different
  obligations. Evaluate the actual operation, not a generic license label.

When the boundary is unresolved, stop the dependent derivation, preserve source
identity, and request the needed rights or owner decision. Do not “rewrite”
uncleared expression as a shortcut.

## Compact provenance record

```text
Source URL and exact revision/release:
License identity, location, and covered scope:
Copied/adapted material:
Required copyright, attribution, license, and notices:
Distribution/linking obligations:
Compatibility decision and owner, if needed:
```

Keep it with the repository's existing provenance records. Preserve research
source identity even when only facts inform an independent implementation.
Do not create a second ledger when the existing one answers the question.
