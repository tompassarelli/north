# Harness design for coding agents (arXiv 2609.20804)

- Authors: Run-Ze Fan, Zihao Zhang, Simin Ma, Yebowen Hu, Shouju Wang, Kaiqiang Song, Fei Liu, Hamed Zamani, Xiaoyang Wang
- Title: An Empirical Study of Harness Design for Coding Agents
- arXiv: 2609.20804v1 [cs.AI], 17 September 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804

- Merged 2026-10-12 from Claude Opus and Codex gpt-6-astra readings, with disagreements checked against the paper and the worker ledger.

## What it measured

A fixed ReAct harness varied context management (T0 none, ending the run on overflow; T1 elide stale tool output; T2 add a recall tool; T3 LLM summary; T4 elide at 60% of the window, summarize at 85%), a plan re-injected each turn, and predefined tools against bash only. Four open models (Nemotron-3 30B, 120B, 550B; Mistral-Medium-3.5-128B) ran SWE-Bench Verified (500 Python tasks) and Terminal-Bench 2.1 (89) at 32k–128k: 176 settings, one run per task. Planning and tools were varied only at T4/128k.

## Findings

1. Context management mostly prevents overflow stops: the managed tiers' gain over T0 falls from 35.7 to 2.7 points on SWE-Bench (9.5 to 2.8 on Terminal-Bench) from 32k to 128k, tracking T0's overflow rate.
2. Managed tiers succeed alike. T4 costs least in 7 of 8 panels: 3.3% below T3 at 32k, 13.1% at 128k, at list prices with no cache discount.
3. Recall sits idle: T2 against T1 scores 15 wins, 14 losses, 3 ties (−0.36 points); 36 of 64 recall-enabled settings never call it, and its stubs point to re-reading instead.
4. A plan adds 11.6 points (significant) for 30B, which otherwise stops before editing in 68.6% of runs. For 550B and Mistral it cuts SWE-Bench cost 30–32% and turns 23–24%, mostly verification, at −2.0 and −0.4 points (not significant).
5. Predefined tools add 15.0 (SWE-Bench) and 10.1 (Terminal-Bench) points for 30B. Bash-only gives 550B 3.6 SWE-Bench points (significant) at 53% less cost but costs Mistral 23.2 points there; the arm also changes prompts, read-before-write checks and lint feedback.
6. Stuck detection was fixed, never measured: a reminder at 5 identical calls, a stop at 8 identical failures, permission denials excluded.

## For North and Socrates

Socrates' models sit at or beyond the strongest model here, on harder benchmarks, so the strong-model results apply.

Both readings agree: build no recall tool or trajectory judge; North owns none of the varied parts, since Codex app-server owns context, compaction and tools (north:docs/architecture.md); single runs of four open models leave our crossovers unplaced.

Where they split, the evidence favours:
- Context, the Opus reading: windows bind for leads, not workers (ledger, 2–12 October 2026: Claude workers 0.00 compactions per run; Plato 11 and most Codex leads and sub-leads 1.2–20). Under prompt caching, eliding old history rewrites the cached prefix, so what transfers is keeping bulk out of context, as the `agents tick-precheck` trial does for lead ticks.
- T4's saving, the Codex reading's 3.3–13.1%, not 5–14%.
- Over-verification, neither the rerun hook nor completion receipts: the rerun counts behind the hook were polls the ledger's check pattern matched. Real reruns of a passed check are near zero, and Codex already refuses them.
- Tools, the Opus reading: workers already send 95.5% of Bash, Read, Edit and Write calls through Bash (1,107 transcripts, 8–12 October).
- Cost, the Codex reading: dollar rankings do not transfer to cached, subscription-billed sessions; nothing shows them reversed either.
